# Opening trials: what they are for

A trial lets you look through an opening you don't own before you unlock
it. It is a guided walk through the opening's variations, not a run, so you
can see how the opening plays out over time. Agreed on 2026-11-17. Not built
yet: when it is, its rules go in `rules/trials.md`, each with a test.

## Intent

- **Free and unlimited.** Trials don't give the experience of a real run,
  so any opening can be tried as often as you like.
- **Nothing counts.** A trial grades nothing, earns no pawns, adds nothing
  to the Playbook, and doesn't count toward the growth curve (games played,
  pace, form or scheduling).
- **Learning is what you buy.** A trial shows you the opening. Playing it
  from memory comes with unlocking it.

## How a trial works

1. **Where it starts.** Select a locked opening on the Playbook tree, and
   **Try** shows beside **Unlock** in the bottom bar. Nothing else starts a
   trial. A locked node the book doesn't name has no Try, since there's no
   opening to walk.
2. **What it walks.** The tried opening is the named opening the node stands
   for, plus every opening the book names under it. The trial starts at move
   one, on the player's side of the tree.
3. **Moves inside the opening.** At each position, the moves on offer are
   the ones that continue at least one line of the opening. This applies to
   both sides' moves. There is no main move: only the opening's variations.
4. **One path plays itself.** When only one move is on offer, it is played
   for you at the usual opponent pace. That covers the moves leading to the
   opening and any single path inside it, on either side.
5. **Branches go in the bottom row.** When more than one move is on offer,
   the board waits. The bottom row lists the moves, most played first, each
   named by the variation it leads to, as growth shows its answers. Tap one
   to play it. Most branches are the opponent's replies.
6. **Past the book.** When no move is on offer, you play on against the
   engine for as long as you want, with nothing recorded, like analysis
   mode.
7. **Going back.** The move list stays tappable. Tap an earlier move to
   return the board there. At a branch point, its variations show again.
8. **Buying.** The trial's top bar always shows **Unlock**. It does exactly
   what Unlock on the tree node does, whatever is on the board, then closes
   the trial and returns to the tree with the opening unlocked. Unlock is
   free today. Once openings cost pawns, it shows the price.
